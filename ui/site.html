01 · The problem

Students ask rule questions. General chatbots guess.

NED's undergraduate rules fill 34 pages across two formal notifications. Students rarely read them, and a general chatbot answers from memory, without a source and without knowing NED's current rules.

General chatbot
"Final exam dene ke liye kam az kam kitni hazri chahiye?"

"Usually between 60% and 75%, it depends on your university."

  • No source to check
  • Not based on NED's current notifications
  • Sounds confident even when wrong (hallucination)
NED Rules Assistant
"Final exam dene ke liye kam az kam kitni hazri chahiye?"

At least 75% attendance in each course EXM 3.5; condonation is possible only with documentary evidence ADM 7.5.

  • Answer comes from NED's official clauses
  • Every fact cites its clause and page
  • Understands Roman Urdu, refuses what the rules don't cover

Retrieval-augmented generation, step by step

    04 · Research design

    The gap we found, and the questions we ask

    We reviewed eight peer-reviewed studies. Each gap below is backed by those papers, and each one leads to a research question.

    Language

    University-rule chatbots were tested only in Polish, Spanish and Indonesian [5-7]. Roman Urdu retrieval exists only for web data [8]. No study tests Roman Urdu questions on university rules.

    Leads to RQ3

    Retriever

    Dense retrieval won on Wikipedia [2], BM25 won on Polish regulations [5], a hybrid was used for Spanish rules [6]. The findings conflict.

    Leads to RQ1

    Refusal and trust

    LLMs rarely reject questions their sources can't answer [4], and users trusted a rule chatbot far less than its automatic scores suggested [6].

    Leads to RQ2

    Conflicting rules

    NED's two current notifications give different attendance-condonation ranges. None of the reviewed studies handles documents that disagree.

    Part of RQ2
    RQ1

    Which retriever finds the right rule?

    Keyword search (BM25), dense multilingual embeddings or a hybrid: which retrieves the correct NED clause most often?

    Measured with Recall@1/3/5 and MRR@10, per retriever and per language.

    RQ2

    Does grounding reduce wrong answers?

    How much does answering from retrieved clauses reduce incorrect and unsupported answers compared with the same LLM without retrieval, and can the system refuse out-of-scope questions?

    Measured with answer correctness, faithfulness (RAGAS), citation accuracy, refusal rate and McNemar's test.

    RQ3

    How much does Roman Urdu hurt, and can we fix it?

    How much does quality drop when the same questions are asked in Roman Urdu, and how much does a light normalisation step recover?

    Measured as the drop from English to Roman Urdu, with and without normalisation.

    05 · Pilot results

    What the prototype already shows

    A pilot on 25 English questions, the same 25 in Roman Urdu, and 5 unanswerable question pairs. It shows the method works. It is a small test set written by our group, not a final result.

    Retrieval quality by method

    Show the numbers as a table
    Recall@5 for Roman Urdu with BM25, before and after normalisation. The word list closes the gap with English.
    MRR@10 on English: BM25 against a small multilingual embedding model. Keywords win on formal rules, as PoliChat [5] also found.
    Out-of-scope questions refused. A question like "When does Fall 2026 start?" uses words that exist in the rules, so word matching is not enough. This motivates RQ2.

    EXM 3.5 · Notification 56434 · 26-06-202470% to 75%

    A finding from NED's own documents. The two current notifications disagree. A chatbot that silently picks one could mislead a student, so our assistant shows both clauses with their dates and asks the student to confirm with the department.
